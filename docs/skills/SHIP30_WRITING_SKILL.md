# Skill: Ship 30 for 30–Style Grounded Essay

## Purpose
Turn a user’s question and retrieved Lenny’s Podcast/Newsletter evidence into a useful, approximately 1,250-word essay. This is a reusable product capability, not just a general chat instruction.

## Important source rule
The assignment says to read the Ship 30 for 30 guide linked in the original brief and encode its relevant writing principles. Before claiming this skill faithfully reflects the guide, locate and read that exact linked source. Record the actual URL and refine this document based on what it says. The checklist below is an initial assignment-derived specification, not a claim that every item is a quotation or official Ship 30 rule.

## Inputs
- User’s requested topic and audience.
- Retrieved transcript excerpts/chunks with title, URL/path and metadata.
- Any relevant context from the current chat session.
- Desired output format (default: Markdown).

## Writing workflow
1. **Check evidence first.** Identify the key claims the retrieved transcript evidence actually supports. If evidence is insufficient, ask for a narrower topic or explain the limitation.
2. **Choose one central idea.** Avoid turning the essay into a list of unrelated facts.
3. **Open with a hook.** Start with a concrete tension, surprising supported observation, useful question, or relatable problem. Do not invent a personal anecdote or unsupported statistic.
4. **Build a clear progression.** Move from problem/context to insight, explanation, implications, and practical application. Use transitions so the essay feels connected.
5. **Make it skimmable.** Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, selective bold emphasis, and bullets only where they improve clarity.
6. **Use evidence responsibly.** Attribute ideas to the relevant transcript or speaker. Do not fabricate quotes, titles, URLs, statistics, or source references. Clearly separate a source-backed claim from your own synthesis.
7. **Make it useful.** Include concrete steps, questions, or a takeaway a product/growth practitioner can apply.
8. **End with a memorable conclusion.** Return to the central idea and state the practical implication.
9. **Check length and quality.** Aim for about 1,250 words, unless the user asks for another length. A tolerance of roughly 1,100–1,400 words is a reasonable implementation check, not a claim about an official rule.
10. **Return citations.** Include source titles/links or a compact “Sources” section using only retrieved source metadata.

## Output checklist
- [ ] Approximately 1,250 words.
- [ ] Strong, relevant opening.
- [ ] One clear central idea.
- [ ] Logical narrative progression.
- [ ] Descriptive headings and readable paragraphs.
- [ ] Selective bold emphasis/bullets.
- [ ] Specific practical takeaway.
- [ ] Claims supported by transcript evidence.
- [ ] No fabricated quotes, statistics or citations.
- [ ] Clear acknowledgement if the knowledge base is insufficient.

## Safety and quality
- Do not copy the guide or reproduce a living writer’s distinctive phrasing.
- Do not pretend the essay is a transcript or a direct quote.
- If the user asks for unsupported information, say what the indexed sources do and do not establish.
- Keep this skill independent from provider selection so it works with either configured model.
